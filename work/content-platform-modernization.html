Content Platform Modernization

Type: Content Strategy / Product Management Focus: Governance & Cost Sustainability

Before: Legacy self-hosted platform

After: readme.io

Same content mission, rebuilt on a platform two non-technical writers run independently.

The Problem

Rackspace's documentation platform ran on a self-hosted stack (GitHub, Netlify, and Jekyll) that required GitHub proficiency to update, a hard barrier for non-technical writers. Over time it became a dumping ground: internal docs, API references, and even Marketing's company blog were all shoe-horned in because it was the only system available, not because it served any of those content types well. It cost roughly $200K a year to operate and needed a 12-person team of writers and content strategists to run. When company downsizing eliminated that team, I inherited the platform with 2 non-technical writers and an immediate constraint: whatever came next had to work for them on day one.

The Solution

Fixed the content before choosing a platform. Migrating to a new tool without fixing the underlying content strategy would have counted as failure, even if the tool worked well.

  • Content strategy first: Eliminated stale content, then defined a simple ownership test, what should Rackspace actually own versus what already exists elsewhere on the internet, so non-expert writers could make keep/cut calls without subject-matter expertise. Cut the library from 15,000 to 8,000 pieces before evaluating a single platform.
  • Platform selection second: Trialed three CMS platforms and selected readme.io for its simple interface, built-in markdown support (a direct migration path from the existing files), and robust API documentation.
  • Governance: Assigned responsible owners per content section, and partnered with Marketing to move their company blog onto the corporate .com site so they could own and publish their own content directly, instead of routing through a docs platform and team with no stake in it.

Key Impact

  • Cost: Reduced platform operating cost from roughly $200K/year to under $2K/year, a ~99% reduction.
  • Sustainability: Replaced a 12-person specialist team with 2 non-technical writers who operate the platform independently today.
  • Quality: Cut the content library nearly in half by applying a differentiator-based ownership test, not an arbitrary reduction.
  • Ongoing ownership: Continue to own the platform, its content model, and its vendor relationship today, including investment planning across the broader docs, analytics, and design vendor portfolio.
